The four-level plan

Short sessions, three to five times a week. Move up a level when every drill at your current level feels steady on both legs.

Train next to a wall or counter. Stop if you feel sharp pain. If you're recovering from an injury, get a clinician's OK first.

    3 Two rings

    Reaches pull you off center; your ankle brings you back.

    • Single-leg reaches3 × 8 each
    • Mini squats3 × 10
    • Single-leg balance3 × 45 s

    4 Full stack

    Take away vision or add a moving target.

    • Eyes-closed balance3 × 20 s
    • Catch and throw3 × 30 s
    • Single-leg squats3 × 6

Move up only when you meet the goal on both legs.

Your first four weeks

A starting point. Stay on a week longer if it isn't steady yet.

  1. Week 1

    Level 1, three sessions. Learn the drills with the wall close.

    About 6 min per session

  2. Week 2

    Level 1, four sessions. Try single-leg without touching the wall.

    About 8 min per session

  3. Week 3

    Level 2 if you met the level 1 goal. Otherwise, repeat week 2.

    About 8 min per session

  4. Week 4

    Level 2, four sessions. Add head turns and toe taps.

    About 10 min per session

The research behind it

Balance training is one of the better-studied ways to reduce ankle sprains, particularly repeat sprains. Programs in the research are short, frequent, and progressive, which is how this plan is built.

  • McGuine TA, Keene JS. The effect of a balance training program on the risk of ankle sprains in high school athletes. Am J Sports Med, 2006.
  • Hupperets MDW, Verhagen EALM, van Mechelen W. Effect of unsupervised home based proprioceptive training on recurrences of ankle sprain. BMJ, 2009.
