  • A midfielder who kept rolling one ankle

    Maya R., club soccer

    The problem
    Sprained the same ankle three seasons in a row.
    What changed
    Ten minutes on the disc before every practice, moving up a level every three weeks.
    Result
    Played every game of her spring season without an ankle injury.

  • A trail runner coming back from time off

    Derek L., trail runner

    The problem
    Felt unsteady on rocky descents after a year away from running.
    What changed
    Level 1 and 2 drills on rest days, three times a week.
    Result
    Finished his first 25K back without turning an ankle.

  • A coach warming up a whole team

    Coach Alvarez, high school basketball

    The problem
    Needed a quick ankle routine that worked for every player.
    What changed
    Four stations at different levels, rotating every two minutes during warm-ups.
    Result
    Warm-ups take the same time, and every player now trains ankles twice a week.

  • A lifter who wanted steadier single-leg work

    Priya S., powerlifter

    The problem
    Ankles wobbled at the bottom of split squats and step-ups.
    What changed
    Level 3 mini squats added to warm-ups before every leg day.
    Result
    Split squats feel locked in, and she added load for the first time in months.

  • A hiker getting ready for a big season

    Tom K., backpacker

    The problem
    Worried about loose rock on long descents with a heavy pack.
    What changed
    The four-week plan, then level 4 twice a week before trips.
    Result
    Hiked a five-day section of the John Muir Trail with no rolled ankles.

  • A parent back in pickup basketball

    Janelle W., rec league

    The problem
    Hadn't played in years and didn't want to get hurt coming back.
    What changed
    Levels 1 and 2 at home, a few minutes each evening for a month.
    Result
    Back on the court every Thursday and feels steadier landing rebounds.
