How to start

  1. Set up by a wall

    Put the base on a flat floor within arm's reach of a wall or counter.

  2. Hold the level 1 drills

    Two-foot, then single-leg balance. Aim for steady, not perfect.

  3. Add a ring when it's easy

    When you can hold every drill at a level with control, stack the next ring.

Five minutes. Every warm-up.

Before practice, before a run, before leg day. Short, frequent sessions are what build steadier ankles.

Questions

How long does a session take?

Most people do 5–10 minutes, three to five times a week. It fits easily into a warm-up.

Can I use it with shoes on?

Yes. Barefoot is harder and works the small muscles in your feet more, so try both once you're comfortable.

Will it scratch my floors?

No. The base is non-marking on hardwood, tile, and gym flooring.

Is it safe after an ankle sprain?

Balance training is common in sprain recovery, but timing matters. Check with a physical therapist or doctor before you start.
