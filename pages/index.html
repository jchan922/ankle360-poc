Train the reaction, not just the muscle.

Every time the disc tips, the muscles and tendons around your ankle have to catch it. Doing that on purpose, a little harder each week, trains your ankle to react faster when a real misstep happens.

Balance training is one of the better-studied ways to lower the risk of ankle sprains, especially for people who have sprained an ankle before.

ANKLE360 is exercise equipment, not a medical device. If you're recovering from an injury, check with a physical therapist or doctor before you start.

  • McGuine TA, Keene JS. The effect of a balance training program on the risk of ankle sprains in high school athletes. Am J Sports Med, 2006.
  • Hupperets MDW, Verhagen EALM, van Mechelen W. Effect of unsupervised home based proprioceptive training on recurrences of ankle sprain. BMJ, 2009.
